# Peer Comparison Memo — Investment Committee

**Scope:** AAPL, MSFT, NVDA — latest fiscal year available for each company in METRICS.csv.
All figures sourced directly from METRICS.csv; no external data used. This memo is
informational only and contains no buy or sell recommendation.

| Company | Year | Revenue ($bn) | Operating Margin | Net Margin | Revenue Growth |
|---------|------|--------------:|-----------------:|-----------:|---------------:|
| AAPL | 2025 | 416.2 | 32.0% | 26.9% | 6.4% |

*Note: fiscal years are not aligned across the three companies (NVDA's latest year in
the dataset is 2020, five or more years behind the others).*

## Takeaways — Growth and Profitability

1. **MSFT combines the strongest profitability with sustained growth.** In FY2026 it
   posted the highest operating margin (46.8%) and net margin (40.3%) of the group while
   growing revenue 17.8% — the only peer above 30% on both margin measures at this scale.
2. **NVDA's growth is the outlier, but the data is dated.** Its 52.7% revenue growth in
   FY2020 (to $16.7bn) is the fastest in the dataset, yet it also shows the group's only
   revenue decline (FY2019, −6.8%), illustrating high year-to-year volatility; its
   FY2020 margins (27.2% operating, 26.0% net) trail both peers' latest figures.
3. **AAPL is the largest but slowest-growing.** At $416.2bn revenue in FY2025 it dwarfs
   the others, with margins recovering to 32.0% operating / 26.9% net after a dip in
   FY2024 (24.0% net), but its 6.4% growth trails MSFT's by more than 11 points.

## Risks to Watch

1. **Comparability risk from misaligned fiscal years.** NVDA's latest data point in
   METRICS.csv is FY2020; conclusions about its current profitability versus AAPL (FY2025)
   and MSFT (FY2026) rest on stale data and could materially understate its present position.
2. **Growth-to-margin trade-off concentration.** The dataset shows margins vary meaningfully
   year to year (AAPL net margin swung 25.3% → 24.0% → 26.9% over FY2023–FY2025; NVDA
   operating margin fell from 32.5% to 26.1% in FY2019), so single-year snapshots may not
   be representative of structural profitability.
