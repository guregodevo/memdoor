### NOTES
The essential simplification is that the fixed path edges 1→2→…→2N make strong connectivity equivalent to a prefix-crossing condition: every proper prefix must receive at least one added edge from the suffix to the prefix. In matching language, after ordering the black vertices and white vertices, a prefix with `a` black vertices and `c` white vertices is closed exactly when the first `a` black vertices are all matched to white indices at most `c`. Thus the problem becomes counting permutations whose prefix maxima stay above a monotone boundary derived from the string, or dually, counting inverse permutations whose running suffix minima stay below a monotone boundary.

The core difficulty is not the graph-theoretic reduction but the counting step: the boundary is an arbitrary monotone sequence of length up to 2e5, and the natural DP over the current maximum, current minimum, or number of open backward edges is quadratic. The useful structure is that intersections of bad prefix events have counts that factor into falling factorials, producing convolution-like recurrences. The challenge is to evaluate those recurrences efficiently under modulo 998244353, likely using NTT-based divide-and-conquer, block methods, or a more specialized combinatorial formula.

Simple necessary conditions are also important: if the first vertex is white, the first prefix has no black head and the answer is 0; if the last vertex is black, the last proper prefix has no white tail and the answer is 0. These early exits avoid edge cases in boundary definitions.

### NEXT
- First-bad-prefix recurrence + CDQ/NTT: define `h_a` as the number of white vertices before the `(a+1)`-st black vertex, with sentinel `h_N = N`. Count partial matchings whose first closed prefix is exactly `a`; this gives a triangular recurrence with falling-factorial terms. Scale by factorials to turn the sum into convolution queries against the factorial sequence, then use divide-and-conquer over `a` and NTT to add left-half contributions to right-half queries. Pitfalls: invalid terms when `h_a < a`, off-by-one in the sentinel and query index, need to truncate NTT lengths to the relevant `h` range, and Python NTT performance may be heavy.
- Inverse running-minimum inclusion-exclusion (the supplied plan): use the inverse permutation from whites to blacks, reveal whites from right to left, and require the running minimum to stay below a nonincreasing boundary derived from the string. Bad events are “the first `u` revealed values all lie after the boundary”; their intersections factor over intervals, giving a DP over the largest selected bad event with falling-factorial block weights. Compute the convolution-like DP with CDQ/NTT and combine with factorial tails. Pitfalls: boundary off-by-one, especially for the zero-white prefix; impossible events when the available right-side black count is smaller than `u`; inclusion-exclusion signs and final combination are easy to get wrong.
- Queue/open-edge DP and polynomial-operator formulation: scan vertices left to right and let `U` be the number of backward-open pairs (black in prefix matched to white in suffix). The color balance determines the forward-pending count, so the state is one-dimensional. Transitions at black/white vertices are local with weights involving `U` and the balance, and strong connectivity is exactly `U > 0` after every proper prefix. This can be expressed as applying differential operators to a polynomial. Try to exploit the operator algebra, segment trees of transformations, or a CDQ formulation. Pitfalls: naive DP is O(N^2), the projection to `U > 0` complicates operator composition, and invalid states with negative forward-pending count must be excluded.
- Block/sqrt decomposition of the factorial recurrence: group indices with the same boundary value; within a group, contributions from earlier indices in the same group become simple running prefix sums. For contributions from previous groups, maintain block-level convolutions or direct block sums to answer base queries. This gives a simpler but slower O(N sqrt N)-type algorithm. Pitfalls: likely too slow in Python for 2e5 if not heavily optimized, memory can blow up if full block convolutions are stored, and block-size tuning is delicate; useful as a fallback or for medium-size validation.
- Determinant / rook-polynomial / nonintersecting-paths route: view the permutation matrix and bad prefix events as emptiness of certain rectangles, or as a path staying above a boundary. Investigate whether the first-bad decomposition yields a Hessenberg, Toeplitz, or Hankel determinant, a Ferrers-board rook polynomial, or an LGV determinant that can be evaluated with fast polynomial methods. Pitfalls: the rectangles are not simply nested, generic determinants are O(N^3), the special structure may not exist or may reduce to the same CDQ convolution, and proving the formula is nontrivial.
- Hybrid/special-case and brute-force validation: add early exits for first white or last black, handle `N = 1`, detect long runs where the boundary is constant (which can be processed in linear time within the run), and detect the diagonal case where the recurrence becomes the classical indecomposable-permutation convolution solvable with one NTT/FPS inversion. Use O(N^2) DP or brute force for small `N` to validate formulas. Pitfalls: special cases may not cover the worst-case arbitrary boundary, hybrid transitions must avoid double counting, and brute force is only for testing, not the final solution.
