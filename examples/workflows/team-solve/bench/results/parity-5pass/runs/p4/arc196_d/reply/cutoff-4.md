The attempt was moving from tuning the existing CDQ solver toward a faster crossing-detection sweep: for each sign, process intervals in descending original index and maintain unresolved later intervals in heaps by right endpoint at left-coordinate leaves, with a segment tree of heap-top maxima to range-query and pop all intervals crossed by the current one, plus a mirrored pass for the opposite crossing orientation. It established the overall reduction to computing `f[j] = max bad previous index`, answering queries by prefix maxima, and verified the strict crossing conditions and the descending-index invariant that the first reported crossing partner for a later interval is its maximum such partner. It also flagged the naive dynamic 2D Fenwick/segment-tree route as likely too slow in Python and leaned toward the heap-sweep alternative. It had not yet produced final code, benchmarks, or a decision between this sweep and a tuned CDQ. Remaining work is to implement the two crossing passes plus same-left/same-right endpoint updates, integrate query answering, add pruning/early breaks, test worst-case random/laminar/crossing inputs, and compare performance against the tuned CDQ.
