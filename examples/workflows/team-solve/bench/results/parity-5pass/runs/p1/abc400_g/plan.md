Reduce the problem to choosing 2K cakes and assigning each chosen cake to one of the three attributes, requiring an even number of chosen cakes for each attribute; the maximum assigned sum equals the maximum total pair price. Solve the unconstrained version by taking the 2K cakes with largest max-attribute value and assigning each to a maximizing attribute, giving a baseline total and a parity mask. A parity-exchange argument shows that an optimal parity-feasible assignment can be obtained from this baseline by at most two local changes, each either recoloring a selected cake or swapping a selected cake with an unselected one. Therefore the answer is the baseline total minus the minimum loss needed to make the parity mask zero using 0, 1, or 2 such changes. Precompute the cheapest recolor and swap operations for each parity mask, plus top-two selected and unselected values to enforce disjointness, and evaluate the constant number of two-operation combinations.
